The guarantee

Secrets are stripped before the model ever sees them.

Redaction runs at the gateway, on your machine, on the path out. Tool output and terminal results are scanned and rewritten before a single token is streamed to the agent or sent to any provider. The model reasons over <<REDACTED_*>> placeholders — never the real value.

  • Egress-side, not trust-side. The secret is removed before it can be reasoned over, logged, cached by a provider, or leaked — not merely promised to be discarded later.
  • IPs, emails, API keys, tokens are detected and replaced with distinctive tokens like <<REDACTED_IP_ADDRESS>>.
  • Enforced by the agent, too. IDEAgent will not write a redacted token back into a file verbatim, so a redacted value can't silently re-enter your codebase through an edit.

"Why not just use X?"

If your code is allowed to leave the machine, plenty of great tools exist. IDEAgent is for when it isn't.

Why not Copilot / Cursor / Windsurf?

They send your code — or the context they build from it — to a vendor cloud to do the work. For a codebase under an egress ban, that transfer is the exact thing you're not allowed to do, no matter how good the retention policy is. IDEAgent does the retrieval on your machine and redacts what's left before anything leaves.

Why not "zero-retention" enterprise plans?

Zero-retention is a promise about what a provider does after receiving your data — your code still crossed the boundary. Many contracts and regulations prohibit the transfer itself, not just the storage. Redaction-before-egress and local-only mode remove the transfer, so there's nothing to retain.

Why not paste into ChatGPT?

Manual pasting is exactly the uncontrolled, unaudited egress your policy exists to stop — secrets and client identifiers go out with the snippet, and nothing enforces what you paste. IDEAgent keeps a human in the loop but strips secrets automatically and mechanically.

Why not a local model in a generic IDE?

Nothing wrong with it — a truly local model keeps context on the machine, which is exactly what we enforce. But a bare local model gives you no on-device retrieval and no safety net the day you're allowed to reach for a stronger remote model like Claude or GPT. IDEAgent is the whole path: local retrieval, egress-side redaction when you do reach out, HITL, and free choice of provider — so "local" is the default, not the ceiling.

The components that keep it on your machine

The redaction guarantee isn't a feature bolted onto a cloud tool — it falls out of running the whole stack in your own environment. These are the parts that make on-device context and egress-side redaction possible, and where to read how each works. Fully local works too: run Ollama for provable zero egress, though matching API-level models takes high-end hardware.
